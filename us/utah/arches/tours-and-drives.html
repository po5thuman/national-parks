Arches National Park Tours & the 18-Mile Scenic Drive

Arches is one paved road. Eighteen miles from the entrance station to Devils Garden, two short spurs, and a dozen pull-outs that between them contain most of what people come to see. You can drive it in 45 minutes or spend a full day on it. This guide covers every stop in order, the guided 4x4 and shuttle options if you would rather not drive, and where to stand when the light goes.

By Maya Ellison Published Aug 26, 2026 Updated Aug 28, 2026 14 min read

2026 Access: Drive In Any Time

No timed-entry reservation is required for Arches in 2026. You can drive through the entrance station at any hour during operating hours with a valid pass. What has not changed is parking — lots at the Windows Section, Delicate Arch and Devils Garden saturate between 8 a.m. and 2 p.m. in season, and rangers hold traffic at the gate when they fill. Start the scenic drive before 7 a.m. or after 2 p.m.

The Scenic Drive: What It Actually Is

The Arches Scenic Drive is a single dead-end road climbing from the entrance station at 4,085 feet up a set of switchbacks and then running north along the spine of the park. Two spurs branch off it: the Windows Section road at mile 11.7 and the Delicate Arch Viewpoint road at mile 14.1.

There is no loop and no through-route. Whatever you drive out, you drive back — which sounds tedious and is not, because the light on the return is completely different and the formations read differently from the opposite direction.

How long to allow

  • 45–60 minutes — driving out and back with no stops. Genuinely not worth doing.
  • 2–3 hours — the main pull-outs, Balanced Rock, and a quick look at the Windows. The realistic minimum.
  • 4–5 hours — all pull-outs plus Double Arch and the Windows loop on foot.
  • Full day — the above plus Delicate Arch or Landscape Arch.

The four stops most people skip — and shouldn’t

Moab Fault Overlook at mile 1.0 is the first pull-out and almost everyone drives past it in a hurry to get in. It explains the entire park: you are looking at 2,500 feet of vertical displacement where the rock layers on one side sit far below their match on the other.

Petrified Dunes at mile 5.0 is a field of fossilised sand dunes — actual Jurassic dunes turned to stone — with the La Sal Mountains behind. In late afternoon it is one of the best photographs in the park and there is usually nobody there.

Salt Valley Overlook looks across the collapsed salt dome whose slow dissolution created every arch in the park. Skyline Arch at mile 16.7 dropped a boulder the size of a house in 1940, doubling its opening overnight.

Drive Out First, Stop on the Way Back

A tactic that works well in peak season: drive the full 18 miles to Devils Garden without stopping, hike or walk at the north end while the lower pull-outs are jammed, then work the stops in reverse on the way out. You get the busy end early and the roadside viewpoints in better afternoon light.

Guided Tours: When They Make Sense

Arches is easy to self-drive and most visitors should. A guided tour earns its cost in three specific situations: you have no vehicle, you want terrain a rental car cannot legally or safely reach, or you want interpretation from someone who knows the geology.

What a guided tour cannot do

No commercial operator can skip a queue, reserve you a parking space, or get you into Fiery Furnace without the same Recreation.gov permit everyone else needs. Any operator implying otherwise is overselling.

4x4 Slickrock Tours

Moab is the four-wheel-drive capital of the United States, and the industry here is mature, professional and genuinely good. Most operators run open-top Jeeps with a driver-guide, three to four hours, from around $120 to $180 per person.

An important distinction: most commercial 4x4 tours run on BLM land outside the park — Hell’s Revenge, Fins & Things, the Gemini Bridges area. These are spectacular and considerably more dramatic than anything inside Arches, but they are not Arches. Read the itinerary carefully if the park itself is what you want.

Inside the park: Willow Springs Road

The one genuine 4x4 route within Arches is Willow Springs Road, branching west near Balanced Rock. It is deep sand, high-clearance four-wheel-drive only, and it leads to Eye of the Whale Arch. A handful of operators run it, and if you want to do it yourself, read our dedicated guide first — this road strands rental vehicles every season.

Rental Car Agreements Prohibit Unpaved Roads

Almost every standard rental agreement in Utah voids your insurance the moment you leave pavement. Willow Springs Road and Salt Valley Road are both unpaved, and recovery from deep sand costs $500–$1,500 out of pocket with no coverage. If you want backcountry terrain, either book a guided 4x4 tour or rent a purpose-built vehicle from a Moab off-road outfitter that explicitly permits dirt roads.

Getting Around Without a Car

Arches has no park shuttle system. This surprises people who have visited Zion, where the shuttle is mandatory in season. In Arches, if you have no vehicle you have three options.

  • Commercial shuttles — several Moab outfitters run scheduled trailhead drops, typically $45–$75 per person. Best for hikers who want a point-to-point drop at Devils Garden.
  • Guided tours — the simplest solution if you want the whole park in one go with no logistics.
  • Bicycle — legal on all paved roads, and the $15 individual entry fee applies. Be realistic: 36 miles round trip, no shade, sustained climbs, and vehicle traffic on a road with narrow shoulders. Strong riders only, and not in summer.

Sunset & Photography Viewpoints

Arches faces broadly west, which means the whole park lights up in the last hour of the day. Four locations stand out, and they suit different amounts of effort.

Delicate Arch — the classic, at a cost

The arch faces west-southwest and takes full sun until the moment it drops. It is the best sunset in the park and you will share it with a hundred people. Arrive 90 minutes early to claim a position on the bowl rim, and carry a headtorch for the slickrock descent in the dark.

Balanced Rock — the easiest good one

A 0.3-mile paved loop, thirty yards from the car park, and the formation glows against the La Sals. If you want a genuinely excellent sunset with no hiking whatsoever, this is it.

The Windows — for sunrise, not sunset

North and South Window face east. At sunrise you can stand inside Turret Arch and shoot through the opening toward the rising sun, which is the single best composition in the park. At sunset this section is backlit and flat.

Fiery Furnace Viewpoint — the underrated one

No permit needed for the overlook, and at sunset the fins turn the deep red that gave the formation its name. It is a roadside stop, it takes twenty minutes, and it is usually near-empty while everyone else queues at Delicate Arch.

Stay After Dark

Arches is an International Dark Sky Park and the road stays open 24 hours. After sunset the crowds vanish within thirty minutes and the Milky Way is visible to the naked eye from any pull-out. Bring a jacket — desert temperatures drop 30°F after dark, even in summer.

Where to Stay Nearby

Combining Arches With Canyonlands

The Island in the Sky district of Canyonlands is 40 minutes from the Arches entrance station, and most full-day guided tours cover both. If you are self-driving, the pairing works well: Arches at dawn for the light and the parking, Canyonlands in the afternoon when the mesa-top viewpoints are at their best and crowds are thinner.

A single $80 America the Beautiful pass covers both, against $30 plus $30 paid separately. The Arches hub has the full fee breakdown.

Driving in From Southern Utah?

Moab sits roughly four and a half hours east of Bryce Canyon. If you are running the classic southern Utah route from Las Vegas, Arches works as the final eastern leg of a Mighty 5 loop.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long is the Arches National Park scenic drive?

Eighteen miles each way from the entrance station to Devils Garden, plus two short spur roads. Allow two to three hours minimum with stops, or a full day if you include a major hike.

Do you need a tour to visit Arches National Park?

No. Arches is straightforward to self-drive on a single paved road, and no timed-entry reservation is required in 2026. Tours are worth it if you have no vehicle, want backcountry 4x4 terrain, or want geological interpretation.

Is there a shuttle in Arches National Park?

No. Unlike Zion, Arches has no park shuttle. Private commercial shuttles run from Moab to trailheads for roughly $45–$75 per person, and guided tours provide transport.

Can you drive the Arches scenic drive in a regular car?

Yes. The entire 18-mile scenic drive and both spur roads are paved and suit any passenger vehicle. Only Willow Springs Road and Salt Valley Road require high-clearance four-wheel drive, and most rental agreements prohibit them.

What is the best time of day to drive through Arches?

Before 7 a.m. or after 2 p.m. Both windows avoid the parking saturation that builds mid-morning, and both give far better light than midday, when the sun flattens the red rock completely.

Are there guided tours of Fiery Furnace?

Yes. Ranger-led Fiery Furnace walks run roughly three hours at $16 per adult and must be booked through Recreation.gov. They sell out quickly in spring and autumn. No commercial operator can substitute for the permit requirement.
