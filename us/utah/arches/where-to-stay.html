Where to Stay Near Arches National Park

There are no hotels inside Arches National Park — none, at any price. Your options are one 51-site campground within the boundary and the town of Moab five miles south, which absorbs almost every visitor to both Arches and Canyonlands. This guide covers every realistic place to sleep, what each actually costs in 2026, and how far ahead you need to book before the good options disappear.

By Daniel Reyes Published Aug 24, 2026 Updated Aug 28, 2026 13 min read

The Short Answer

Stay in north Moab if you want hotel comfort — it puts you 5–7 minutes from the entrance station, which matters enormously now that arriving before 7 a.m. is the main strategy for beating parking congestion. Book Devils Garden Campground if you want to be at Delicate Arch for sunrise without a 5 a.m. alarm and a drive. Everything else is a compromise on distance or price.

There Are No Hotels Inside Arches National Park

This is the single most common misconception about the park, and it is worth stating plainly. Unlike Zion with its lodge, or Yellowstone with its village network, Arches has no in-park accommodation of any kind apart from Devils Garden Campground. No lodge, no cabins, no concession hotel.

The park is also small and linear enough that this barely matters. The entrance station sits five miles north of Moab town centre on US-191. From a north Moab hotel you can be through the gate in under ten minutes — faster than the drive from Devils Garden Campground to the Windows Section, in fact.

Devils Garden Campground — The Only Option Inside the Park

Fifty-one sites at the far north end of the scenic drive, tucked among sandstone fins and juniper. It is one of the most sought-after campgrounds in the National Park System, and the reason is simple: you wake up already inside Arches, eighteen miles past the queue that forms at the entrance station.

What you get

  • 51 sites — a mix of tent and RV, no hookups anywhere.
  • Flush toilets and potable water — available March through October; water is shut off in winter.
  • Picnic table and fire ring at every site.
  • No showers, no dump station, no store. All of those are in Moab.
  • RV limit 40 feet, and sites 1–24 suit tents considerably better.

Reservations Are Mandatory — and Brutally Competitive

Devils Garden Campground is reservation-only from March 1 through October 31, booked exclusively through Recreation.gov. Sites release on a rolling six-month window and spring and autumn dates are gone within minutes of release at 10 a.m. Eastern. There are no walk-up sites in season and rangers do check. Note that this requirement is unchanged by the removal of park timed entry in 2026 — the two systems are entirely separate.

How to actually get a site

Set a calendar reminder for exactly six months before your target date, be logged into Recreation.gov before 10 a.m. Eastern with payment details saved, and have two or three acceptable date combinations ready. Hesitation costs you the booking. If you miss the window, check back frequently — cancellations do appear, particularly two to three weeks out.

Winter is the loophole

From November through February, roughly two dozen sites become first-come, first-served at $15 per night. Nights drop to 20°F and the water is turned off, but you can drive up on a January afternoon and find a site with the whole campground nearly empty. For anyone equipped for cold camping, this is the easiest way to sleep inside Arches.

Why Sleeping Inside Matters

The Delicate Arch trailhead is a five-minute drive from the campground. Sunrise at the arch — perhaps a dozen people instead of a hundred — becomes a casual decision rather than a 4:30 a.m. departure from town.

Moab: Which End of Town?

Moab is essentially one long street — US-191, called Main Street through town — running north to south for about four miles. Where you sit on that line determines your morning, and most booking sites will not tell you this.

North Moab — closest to Arches

Everything north of about 500 North puts you 5–7 minutes from the entrance station. For a park where the whole strategy is getting through the gate before 7 a.m., this is worth paying for. The trade-off is that you will drive to dinner rather than walk.

Typical 2026 shoulder rates run $160–$240 for a mid-range chain or independent. Book three to five months ahead for spring and October.

Central Moab — walkable, 10–14 minutes out

The stretch around Center Street and Main puts you within walking distance of most of Moab’s restaurants, breweries and outfitters. After a long day on slickrock, being able to walk to dinner and leave the car parked has genuine value.

You pay for it with an extra five to seven minutes each morning, and Main Street traffic between 8 and 10 a.m. in peak season is slower than the map suggests.

South Moab — cheapest, 15+ minutes out

Rates drop noticeably south of town, but you are adding ten minutes each way to every park trip, and you will make that trip at least twice a day. On a four-night stay that is well over two hours of extra driving. Worth it if the saving is real; not worth it for $15 a night.

Luxury Resorts

Moab has developed a genuine high-end tier over the last decade, mostly on the ranch land north of town and along the Colorado River corridor on SR-128. Expect $320–$650 per night in season for pools, spas, on-site restaurants and casitas or suites rather than standard rooms.

The river-corridor properties on SR-128 are the most striking — set beneath 400-foot canyon walls with the Colorado running past — but check the drive time carefully. Some sit 20–25 minutes from the Arches entrance, which erodes the early-arrival advantage entirely.

Glamping and Stargazing Domes

The fastest-growing category around Moab: geodesic domes, safari tents and airstream sites on private land outside town, typically $220–$480 per night. Most include a bed, climate control and a private bathroom — camping comfort without any camping.

The real draw is the sky. Moab sits in one of the darkest regions in the contiguous United States, and the dome properties are positioned well outside town light. Several have transparent ceiling panels over the bed, which is a gimmick that turns out to be entirely worth it.

Free and Low-Cost Camping on BLM Land

Grand County is surrounded by Bureau of Land Management land, and dispersed camping is permitted in designated areas — free or up to about $20 per night at developed BLM sites. The Colorado River corridor on SR-128 and the Kane Creek area southwest of town are the established options.

These are first-come, first-served with no reservations, which makes them the fallback when everything else is full. Be genuinely self-sufficient: most sites have a vault toilet at best, no water, and no rubbish collection.

Dispersed Camping Rules Are Enforced

Camping is permitted only in designated dispersed sites around Moab — not anywhere you find a flat spot. Pulling off a dirt road onto undisturbed desert damages cryptobiotic soil crust that takes decades to regrow, and BLM rangers issue citations. Fire restrictions are common from May through October and are strictly enforced. Check current BLM notices before you go.

When to Book

Moab operates on a sharper seasonal curve than almost any town its size. There are two distinct peaks and two genuine bargains.

  • March–May — peak. Book hotels 3–5 months ahead. Spring break week is the single hardest period of the year.
  • June–August — hot, but busy with international visitors. 2–3 months ahead.
  • September–October — the second peak, and October is the best month in the park. Book 4–6 months ahead.
  • November–February — quiet. Rates fall 40–50% and you can often book days ahead.

One local factor worth knowing: Moab hosts several large events, including the Easter Jeep Safari, that fill every room within 40 miles. If your dates fall near Easter, book well before you would otherwise think necessary.

Frequently Asked Questions

Are there hotels inside Arches National Park?

No. Arches has no lodge, cabins or hotel of any kind within its boundary. The only in-park accommodation is Devils Garden Campground. All hotel lodging is in Moab, five miles south of the entrance station.

What is the closest place to stay to Arches National Park?

Devils Garden Campground is inside the park itself. For hotels, north Moab properties along US-191 above 500 North are closest at 5–7 minutes from the entrance station.

Do you need a reservation for Devils Garden Campground?

Yes, from March 1 through October 31, booked through Recreation.gov on a rolling six-month window. Sites sell out within minutes of release. From November through February a portion of sites become first-come, first-served at $15 per night.

How far is Moab from Arches National Park?

The entrance station is five miles north of Moab town centre on US-191 — about 10 minutes from central Moab, or 5–7 minutes from the north end of town. Note the scenic drive then runs a further 18 miles to Devils Garden inside the park.

How far in advance should I book Moab hotels?

Three to five months for spring, four to six for October, and two to three for summer. Winter is genuinely flexible — you can often book a few days out at 40–50% below peak rates.

Is it cheaper to stay in Green River or Monticello?

Yes, typically $75–$120 versus $160+ in Moab. Both sit 50–60 minutes from the Arches entrance, which means two extra hours of driving per day. It works as last-minute overflow, not as a plan.

Can you camp for free near Arches?

Yes, in designated dispersed camping areas on BLM land around Moab, particularly along the SR-128 river corridor and Kane Creek. These are first-come, first-served with minimal facilities, and camping outside designated sites is prohibited and ticketed.
