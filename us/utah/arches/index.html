Arches National Park

The densest concentration of natural stone arches anywhere on Earth — more than 2,000 documented spans packed into 76,518 acres of Entrada sandstone. This is our complete orientation hub: an area-by-area map of the park, 2026 entrance fees, the parking strategy that actually works, and links to every trail, tour and campsite we have tested on the ground.

2026 Entry Update: No Timed-Entry Reservation Required

Arches National Park does not require a timed-entry reservation in 2026. You may enter at any time during operating hours with a valid park pass — no advance booking, no lottery, no ticket. The park does still experience genuine parking saturation between roughly 8 a.m. and 2 p.m. in peak season, so we recommend arriving before 7 a.m. or after 2 p.m. to avoid full lots and entrance station queues. Reservations remain mandatory for Devils Garden Campground and for Fiery Furnace permits, both booked through Recreation.gov.

Orientation

The Arches Map, Area by Area

Arches is a single dead-end road. One paved route runs 18 miles from the entrance station to Devils Garden at the far north end, with two short spurs. Everything below is listed in the order you will reach it driving in — which is the only sensible way to read an Arches map.

Two additional areas sit off the paved road. Willow Springs Road, branching west near Balanced Rock, is the high-clearance 4WD route to Eye of the Whale Arch. The Salt Valley Road heads north toward Klondike Bluffs and Tower Arch — graded dirt, impassable when wet, and closed after storms.

Get the Official Paper Map

The National Park Service hands out a free folded map at the entrance station. Take one even if you have offline maps on your phone — it shows trail junctions, distances and the unpaved road network more clearly than any app, and mobile coverage inside the park is unreliable beyond the visitor centre.

Entrance Fees & Passes

Arches charges per vehicle, not per person, and the pass covers seven consecutive days. If your trip includes any second national park, the annual pass is almost always the cheaper choice.

The arithmetic is simple. A Utah Mighty 5 trip at $30–$35 per park costs $160 in individual entries against $80 for the annual pass. Even Arches plus Canyonlands alone — 40 minutes apart, and both easily done from Moab — reaches $60. Buy the America the Beautiful pass at your first entrance station.

The Real Constraint

Parking & Congestion

With timed entry gone in 2026, parking is the only thing standing between you and the park. Arches has one road, a finite number of spaces at each trailhead, and no shuttle system. When the lots fill, rangers hold traffic at the entrance station until spaces free up.

Peak-Season Entrance Delays

Between roughly 8 a.m. and 2 p.m., March through October, expect queues at the entrance station and full lots at Delicate Arch, the Windows Section and Devils Garden. Delays of 30–60 minutes are routine on spring weekends. No reservation exists to skip this — the only lever you control is arrival time.

The two arrival windows that work

Before 7 a.m. is the strongest option. The entrance station is often unstaffed at that hour (pay on the way out, or use your pass), lots are empty, temperatures are 20–30°F cooler than midday, and the low-angle light is far better for photography than anything you will get at noon.

After 2 p.m. is the practical alternative. The morning wave clears, spaces reopen, and if you time it right you finish at Delicate Arch for sunset — which is the single best two hours in the park.

Parking difficulty by trailhead

  • Delicate Arch (Wolfe Ranch) — the hardest lot in the park. Roughly 100 spaces serving the most popular hike in Utah. Full by 8 a.m. most of the year.
  • Windows Section — a large lot that still fills, because four major arches share it. Turnover is fast; a 10-minute wait usually produces a space.
  • Devils Garden — fills by 9 a.m. in season, and the campground traffic compounds it.
  • Balanced Rock — small, but visits are short and turnover is constant.
  • Sand Dune & Broken Arch — reliably has space even at midday. The park’s best-kept parking secret.

One rule that matters: park only in designated spaces. Roadside parking on the shoulder is prohibited throughout the park, actively ticketed, and damages the fragile soil crust either side of the pavement. If a lot is full, drive on and come back.

Our recommendation

October is the best month in Arches. Daytime highs in the 70s, cold clear nights, stable weather, and crowds that thin noticeably after the first week. March and April come close and are now particularly attractive with no timed-entry requirement — see our March guide to Utah’s Mighty 5 for the full seasonal breakdown.

Avoid committing to summer midday hiking. Surface temperatures on open slickrock exceed 140°F, there is no shade on the Delicate Arch approach, and heat illness is the most common serious incident rangers respond to in this park.

Summer Heat & Flash Flood Notice

June through August, carry a minimum of four litres of water per person per day and finish exposed hikes before 10 a.m. The monsoon runs mid-July through September: brief violent storms can flood washes and make unpaved roads including Willow Springs and Salt Valley impassable within minutes. Never enter a wash when storms are visible anywhere on the horizon.

Still Required

What You Do Need to Book

Timed entry is gone, but two things inside Arches still require an advance reservation through Recreation.gov, and both sell out.

Reservation Required

Fiery Furnace

A maze of sandstone fins with no marked trail. Entry requires either a self-guided permit or a place on a ranger-led walk, both booked in advance. Self-guided permits release on a rolling window; ranger walks are limited and go quickly.

Devils Garden Campground

The only campground inside Arches — 51 sites, 18 miles from the entrance, and the single best way to be at Delicate Arch for sunrise. Bookings open six months ahead and the spring and autumn windows are gone within minutes of release.
