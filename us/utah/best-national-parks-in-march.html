The Best National Parks to Visit in March: Utah’s Mighty 5, Ranked by Real Conditions

March is the single most misjudged month in Utah. Moab sits in perfect hiking weather while Bryce Canyon, just four hours west, is still buried under two feet of snow at 9,000 feet. We ranked all five parks on March conditions our field team recorded across four consecutive seasons.

By Maya Ellison Published Aug 19, 2026 Updated Aug 28, 2026 14 min read

The Short Answer

Arches is the best national park to visit in March — daytime highs near 60°F, full trail access, and the last few weeks before timed-entry reservations resume on April 1. Canyonlands and Capitol Reef follow closely. Zion is workable but wet underfoot. Skip Bryce Canyon unless you specifically want snow photography.

Why March Divides Utah in Half

Utah’s national parks span more than 3,400 feet of elevation. That gap is irrelevant in June and decisive in March. Moab sits at roughly 4,000 feet and thaws early; the Bryce Canyon rim sits above 8,000 feet and holds snowpack into April most years.

The practical effect is that two parks are in full spring hiking season while a third is still running winter road closures — in the same week, on the same trip. Most March itineraries fail because they were built on annual averages instead of elevation.

The three variables that actually matter

Ignore the monthly average temperature published on park websites. It blends rim and canyon-floor readings into a number that describes nowhere. Plan around these instead:

  • Base elevation of the trailhead — not the park’s peak elevation. Delicate Arch starts at 4,300 feet; Bryce’s Navajo Loop starts at 8,000.
  • Overnight low, not daytime high — March lows below freezing mean ice on north-facing switchbacks until roughly 10 a.m., regardless of a pleasant afternoon.
  • Water temperature — relevant only in Zion, where The Narrows runs near 40°F and demands a drysuit, not the neoprene socks that suffice in summer.

Spring Runoff Warning

Late March is peak snowmelt in the Virgin River watershed. The Narrows closes when flow exceeds 150 CFS, and in a heavy snow year that threshold can hold from mid-March through May. Check the USGS gauge the morning you plan to hike — not the night before. Flow can double overnight.

1. Arches National Park — The Clear Winner

Arches takes the top spot for one structural reason beyond the weather: timed-entry reservations do not resume until April 1. For the whole of March you can drive through the entrance station at any hour without a booking — the only month in the main season where that is true.

Conditions cooperate too. Daytime highs near 60°F make the 3-mile Delicate Arch round trip genuinely pleasant rather than the heat-management exercise it becomes by June. The slickrock approach, which bakes to 140°F surface temperature in summer, is comfortable all afternoon.

What to hike in Arches in March

  • Delicate Arch (3 mi round trip, 480 ft gain) — go for sunset; the March crowd at the arch is roughly a third of the May figure.
  • Devils Garden Primitive Loop (7.9 mi) — the full loop is open, and the scrambling sections are dry by mid-month.
  • Fiery Furnace — permit or ranger-led only, and March is the easiest month of the year to secure a slot.

2. Canyonlands National Park — Empty and Underrated

Canyonlands receives fewer than a fifth of Zion’s visitors, and March amplifies that gap. Island in the Sky overlooks that hold thirty people in October hold three in March.

The caveat is unpaved roads. The White Rim Road and the Needles district’s 4WD routes can hold mud and ice into late March, and the Shafer Switchbacks are genuinely dangerous when frozen. Paved access to Grand View Point and Mesa Arch is unaffected.

Mesa Arch at sunrise, without the tripod scrum

The Mesa Arch sunrise shot is one of the most photographed compositions in the American West, and in peak season it means forty photographers three-deep by 5:30 a.m. In March, arriving forty minutes before sunrise is usually enough to claim a front position — and the sun rises at a civilised 6:50 a.m.

3. Capitol Reef National Park — The Quiet One

Capitol Reef draws 1.2 million visitors a year against Zion’s 4.6 million, and most of them arrive between May and September. March visitors get the Scenic Drive, Hickman Bridge and the Cassidy Arch switchbacks in near solitude.

One genuine loss: the Fruita orchards are bare in March. If picking fruit is part of the appeal, that runs June through October and no amount of planning moves it.

4. Zion National Park — Good, With Real Caveats

Zion has the warmest March temperatures of the five parks, and Angels Landing permits are dramatically easier to win than in summer. That combination looks unbeatable on paper. The problem is water.

Late March is peak snowmelt in the Virgin River watershed. The Narrows — the reason a large share of visitors come to Zion at all — closes above 150 CFS, and in a heavy snow year that closure can run from mid-March into May. Book Zion in March for the canyon rim trails, not for the river.

The shuttle question

Zion Canyon Scenic Drive reopens to the mandatory shuttle in early March, though exact dates shift each year. In the narrow window before it starts you can drive your own vehicle up the canyon — a genuinely different experience, and the reason some regulars book the first week of March specifically.

5. Bryce Canyon National Park — Snow Trip Only

Bryce is not a bad March destination. It is a different March destination. At 42°F average high and 19°F average low, the amphitheatre is a winter landscape: orange hoodoos capped with snow under a deep blue high-altitude sky, which is arguably the park’s best photographic condition.

What you give up is hiking. The Navajo Loop’s Wall Street section closes in winter, and the Queens Garden switchbacks demand microspikes most March mornings. Treat Bryce in March as a rim-viewpoint and snowshoe trip, and it delivers. Treat it as a hiking trip and it will disappoint.

Combining Parks in One March Trip

Moab (Arches + Canyonlands) and Springdale (Zion) are 4.5 hours apart. Pairing them in March works well because both are in season. Adding Bryce means accepting a winter park in the middle of a spring itinerary — plan it as a deliberate contrast, or leave it for May.

What to Pack for Utah in March

The defining March problem is the daily temperature swing. A 32°F dawn and a 60°F afternoon in the same six hours means a single insulation layer is always wrong.

  • Microspikes — non-negotiable for Bryce, and useful on Zion’s north-facing switchbacks before 10 a.m.
  • Windproof shell — the Colorado Plateau in March runs sustained 20 mph winds on exposed slickrock.
  • Sun protection — March UV at 5,000 feet with snow reflection burns faster than most visitors expect.
  • Two litres per person minimum — cool weather masks dehydration; the desert humidity is unchanged.

Final Verdict

If you can only visit one national park in March, make it Arches — the last month before timed entry, with full trail access and manageable crowds. If you want solitude above all, take Capitol Reef. Build in Canyonlands as a same-base day trip from Moab, treat Zion as a rim-trail destination, and save Bryce Canyon for May unless snow is the point.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is March a good time to visit Utah national parks?

Yes for the low-elevation parks. Arches, Canyonlands and Capitol Reef are all in comfortable hiking conditions with well below peak crowds. Bryce Canyon remains a winter environment through March.

Do I need a timed-entry reservation for Arches in March?

No. Arches timed entry runs April 1 through October 31. March visitors enter with a standard park pass at any time of day.

Can you hike The Narrows in March?

Sometimes. It depends entirely on Virgin River flow, which must be below 150 CFS. Even when open, water temperature near 40°F makes a drysuit essential rather than optional.

How cold does it get in Utah national parks in March?

Overnight lows run near freezing in Moab (32°F) and well below at Bryce Canyon (19°F). Daytime highs range from 42°F at Bryce to 63°F in Zion Canyon.
