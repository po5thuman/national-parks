National Parks in Utah

Five national parks inside 300 miles of sandstone. Utah canyons run from the 2,000-foot gorge of Zion to the hoodoo amphitheatres of Bryce and the river-carved maze of Canyonlands. This hub is our permanently maintained index: every national park in Utah, plus the permit rules, seasonal timing and road trip routes our Southwest desk tests on the ground.

The Mighty 5

Utah Park Directory

See the driving route

Ranked below by how much planning each one demands, not by popularity. Two of the five now gate entry behind a reservation system, and one of those sells out months ahead.

1
Most Visited

Zion National Park

A 2,000-foot-deep canyon with the Virgin River running through it. Home to Angels Landing and The Narrows, and the strictest permit system in the state.

  • Best SeasonApr–May, Oct
  • Permit RequiredYes — Angels Landing
  • DifficultyModerate–Strenuous
  • Annual Visitors4.6M
Zion Guide Permits
2
Timed Entry

Arches National Park

More than 2,000 catalogued arches inside 76,000 acres. Small, concentrated, and the hardest Utah park to enter on impulse between April and October.

  • Best SeasonMar–May, Sep–Nov
  • Permit RequiredYes — timed entry
  • DifficultyEasy–Moderate
  • Annual Visitors1.8M
Arches Guide March Timing
3
Dark Sky Park

Bryce Canyon National Park

The largest concentration of hoodoos on Earth, perched at 8,000 feet. Cold, high, and spectacular in winter when the rim crowds thin out completely.

  • Best SeasonMay–Jun, Sep–Oct
  • Permit RequiredNo — day use
  • DifficultyEasy–Moderate
  • Annual Visitors2.4M
Bryce Guide Road Trip
4
Backcountry

Canyonlands National Park

Four districts with no connecting roads between them. Island in the Sky is the day trip; The Maze is one of the most genuinely remote places in the lower 48.

  • Best SeasonApr–May, Oct
  • Permit RequiredYes — backcountry
  • DifficultyModerate–Expert
  • Annual Visitors800K
Canyonlands Guide Permits
5
Least Crowded

Capitol Reef National Park

A hundred-mile wrinkle in the Earth’s crust, plus historic Fruita orchards you can still pick from in season. The quietest of the Mighty 5 by a wide margin.

  • Best SeasonApr–Jun, Sep–Oct
  • Permit RequiredNo — day use
  • DifficultyEasy–Moderate
  • Annual Visitors1.2M

Where to Sleep: Springdale & Moab

Zion’s gateway town has roughly 1,300 rooms serving 4.6 million annual visitors. Moab is worse in spring. Reserve Springdale 4–6 months ahead for April and October, and Moab 6–8 months ahead for March through May.

Utah Permit Deadlines You Cannot Miss

The Angels Landing seasonal lottery closes March 1 for spring dates. Arches timed entry releases in three-month blocks at 8 a.m. MT. The Wave (Coyote Buttes North) runs a rolling geolocated daily lottery two days ahead. Every one of these sells out — missing the window means a wasted drive.
