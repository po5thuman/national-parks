Zion National Park

A 2,000-foot-deep canyon with a river running through it. Home to Angels Landing and The Narrows — and the strictest permit system in the state.

  • Best SeasonApr–May, Oct
  • Permit RequiredYes — Angels Landing
  • DifficultyModerate–Strenuous

Bryce Canyon National Park

The largest concentration of hoodoos on Earth, sitting at 8,000 feet. Cold, high, and spectacular in winter when the crowds vanish.

  • Best SeasonMay–Jun, Sep–Oct
  • Permit RequiredNo — day use
  • DifficultyEasy–Moderate

Arches National Park

More than 2,000 catalogued arches packed into 76,000 acres. Small, concentrated, and the hardest Utah park to enter without planning ahead.

Where to Sleep: Springdale & Moab

Zion’s gateway town has roughly 1,300 rooms serving 4.6 million annual visitors. Moab is worse in spring. Our desk tracks availability across both towns weekly — these are the properties that consistently hold inventory closest to the entrance gates.

Booking rule of thumb: reserve Springdale 4–6 months ahead for April and October, and Moab 6–8 months ahead for March through May. Cancellation windows tighten sharply inside 30 days.

Utah Permit Deadlines You Cannot Miss

Angels Landing seasonal lottery closes March 1 for spring dates. Arches timed entry opens in three-month blocks at 8 a.m. MT. The Wave (Coyote Buttes North) runs a rolling geolocated daily lottery two days ahead. Missing any of these means a wasted drive — every one of them sells out.
