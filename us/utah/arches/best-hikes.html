The 5 Best Hikes in Arches National Park

Arches packs more than 2,000 stone spans into 76,518 acres, but the park’s hiking is concentrated into a handful of genuinely excellent routes. These five cover the full range — a two-hour family walk to a seven-mile scramble that ranks among the most technical marked trails in the National Park System. Every distance, time and difficulty rating below comes from our own field measurements, not the trailhead sign.

By Maya Ellison Published Aug 22, 2026 Updated Aug 28, 2026 17 min read

2026 Access Note

No timed-entry reservation is required to enter Arches National Park in 2026. Walk up, drive in, hike. The one exception on this list is Fiery Furnace, which still requires a self-guided permit or a ranger-led booking through Recreation.gov. Parking, not entry, is now the binding constraint — arrive before 7 a.m. or after 2 p.m. See the Arches hub for the full congestion breakdown.

How to choose if you only have one day

Do Delicate Arch at sunrise, drive to the Windows Section for Double Arch and the two Windows before the crowds peak, then finish with Landscape Arch in the afternoon when Devils Garden parking loosens. That is roughly six hours including driving and covers three of the five hikes on this list.

1. Delicate Arch — The One You Came For

It is on the Utah licence plate for a reason. A 46-foot freestanding span perched on the lip of a natural sandstone bowl, with the snow-capped La Sal Mountains 25 miles behind it. No photograph prepares you for the scale or for the way the bowl drops away beneath it.

The trail is 3.2 miles round trip with 629 feet of gain, and it breaks into three distinct sections that catch people out in different ways.

Section 1 — Wolfe Ranch to the slickrock (0.0–0.5 mi)

Flat, gravelled and deceptively easy. You pass the 1906 Wolfe Ranch cabin and cross a footbridge over Salt Wash. There is a short spur to a Ute petroglyph panel on the right — worth the three minutes, and easy to walk straight past.

Section 2 — The slickrock climb (0.5–1.2 mi)

This is the hard part: a broad, unshaded, relentless ramp of bare sandstone with no switchbacks and no markers except occasional cairns. It gains most of the elevation. In summer this section alone accounts for the majority of heat-related rescues in the park.

Section 3 — The ledge (1.2–1.6 mi)

The final approach traverses a rock ledge carved into the cliff face, roughly six feet wide with a drop on the left. It is not technical and there are no chains, but it is genuinely exposed and people with a fear of heights sometimes turn around here. The arch appears with no warning as you round the last corner.

No Shade, No Water, No Shortcuts

There is zero shade on the entire Delicate Arch trail and no water beyond the trailhead tap. Slickrock surface temperature exceeds 140°F on summer afternoons. Carry a minimum of two litres per person, three in summer. Do not attempt this hike between 11 a.m. and 4 p.m. from June through August — start at dawn or go at sunset instead.

Sunrise or sunset?

Sunset is the classic. The arch faces west-southwest and glows deep orange in the last 30 minutes of light. The cost is a crowd — 100+ people in the bowl on a spring evening, and a descent in the dark that requires a headtorch.

Sunrise is the connoisseur’s choice: perhaps a dozen people, cool air on the slickrock climb, and soft indirect light on the arch itself. If you are staying at Devils Garden Campground, the trailhead is a five-minute drive and sunrise becomes trivially easy.

The no-hike alternatives

Two viewpoints on the spur road at mile 14.1 give you the arch without the climb. The Lower Viewpoint is a 100-yard flat walk and shows the arch as a small distant shape. The Upper Viewpoint is a half-mile round trip with 200 feet of gain and a considerably better perspective. Neither replaces standing in the bowl, but both are legitimate options for anyone who cannot manage the main trail.

2. Devils Garden Primitive Loop — The Serious One

The longest maintained trail in Arches and the only one that feels like a genuine backcountry day. The full loop with all spurs runs 7.9 miles and 1,085 feet of cumulative gain, passing eight named arches including Landscape, Partition, Navajo, Double O and Private Arch.

The word “primitive” in the name is doing real work. Past Double O Arch the maintained trail ends and the route becomes cairn-navigated slickrock with several unprotected fin traverses. This is the hardest marked trail in the park.

The three-tier structure

Devils Garden is best understood as three separate hikes stacked on top of each other. You can stop at any tier and still have had an excellent day.

  • Tier 1 — Landscape Arch (1.9 mi RT, easy). Graded gravel, minimal gain, suitable for almost anyone. Turn around here and you have seen the park’s single most impressive span.
  • Tier 2 — Double O Arch (4.2 mi RT, moderate–strenuous). Past Landscape the trail climbs onto the fins. Includes a 30-foot slickrock scramble and one narrow fin walk with drops on both sides.
  • Tier 3 — Full Primitive Loop (7.9 mi, strenuous). Continues past Double O and returns via the unmaintained primitive route. Route-finding required, one genuinely awkward downclimb, and a section that is impassable when wet.

Primitive Loop: Direction and Weather

Hike the loop counter-clockwise — out via Landscape and Double O, back on the primitive trail. Going the other way turns the loop’s worst downclimb into a blind upclimb and puts route-finding at the end of the day when you are tired. The primitive section becomes genuinely dangerous when wet: the slickrock turns greasy and the clay sections are unwalkable. If rain fell in the last 24 hours, do the out-and-back to Double O instead.

Landscape Arch is the reason to start early

The Devils Garden lot serves the campground, the easy walk and the full loop simultaneously, and it fills by 9 a.m. in season. Starting at 7 a.m. gets you a space, cool air for the fin sections, and Landscape Arch without twenty people in the frame.

3. Landscape Arch — The Longest Span in North America

At 306 feet tip to tip, Landscape Arch is the longest natural arch in North America and one of the longest in the world. What the measurement does not convey is how impossibly thin it is — the central span narrows to roughly six feet of rock. It looks like it should not be standing.

In 1991 a 73-foot slab fell from the underside while hikers stood beneath it, and further falls followed in 1995. The trail directly under the arch has been permanently closed since. You now view it from a roped-off area about 100 feet back, which is close enough and considerably safer.

Why we rank it third despite being easy

Pure ratio of reward to effort. It is 1.9 miles round trip on graded gravel with 119 feet of gain — a 45-minute walk that delivers the most structurally astonishing formation in the park. If you have limited mobility, limited time, or children, this is the Arches hike to do.

Two Free Spurs Worth Taking

Just before Landscape Arch, short signed spurs lead to Tunnel Arch and Pine Tree Arch. Together they add about 0.5 miles and fifteen minutes, and almost everyone walks past them. Pine Tree Arch in particular is a genuinely good stop with a framed view through the opening.

4. Fiery Furnace — The Permit Hike

A dense labyrinth of sandstone fins with no marked trail, no signage and no obvious way out. The name is misleading — it refers to the colour of the rock at sunset, not the temperature. The Furnace is actually one of the coolest places in the park, since the fins throw deep shade for most of the day.

This is the only hike in Arches that requires a permit, and the requirement is not bureaucratic caution. People genuinely get lost in here. The fins are 40 to 100 feet high, look near-identical, and GPS is unreliable in the narrow corridors.

Two ways in

  • Ranger-led walk — roughly three hours, $16 per adult, minimum age five. A ranger navigates and interprets. Groups are small and these book out fast.
  • Self-guided permit — $10 per person, and you must watch an orientation video and demonstrate you understand the route-finding and Leave No Trace requirements. Suitable only for hikers confident with unmarked terrain.

Fiery Furnace Permits Are Still Mandatory in 2026

While Arches no longer requires timed entry, Fiery Furnace access remains permit-only through Recreation.gov. Both ranger-led walks and self-guided permits release on a rolling window and sell out within hours in spring and autumn. There is no walk-up option, no standby list, and rangers do check permits at the trailhead. Book before you build your itinerary around it — see our permit tracker for current release timing.

What it is actually like

Physically it is only two to two and a half miles, but the movement is constant: chimneying between fins, dropping into sandy corridors, scrambling up short ledges, squeezing through gaps. Expect to use your hands throughout. The cumulative effort is far higher than the distance implies, which is why we rate it strenuous.

The payoff is Surprise Arch, a handful of unnamed spans, and the strange experience of being enclosed and quiet inside a park where everywhere else is wide open and crowded.

5. Double Arch — The Best Twenty Minutes in the Park

Two enormous spans rising from a shared foundation, the taller reaching 112 feet — the tallest opening in the park. The trail is 0.6 miles round trip on flat packed sand with essentially no elevation gain, and it takes twenty minutes.

What makes it exceptional is that you walk directly underneath and can scramble up onto the sloping rock at the base. Standing inside the structure looking up through both openings is an experience none of the other arches offer, because at every other major span you are held back at a distance.

Do the whole Windows Section while you are there

Double Arch shares a parking area with the Windows loop. For another mile of walking you get North Window, South Window and Turret Arch. The Windows Primitive Loop — a sandy path circling behind the two Windows — takes fifteen extra minutes and is walked by perhaps one visitor in twenty, despite offering the best view of the formations from the rear.

Total for the whole section: about 1.6 miles and 90 minutes. It is the highest-density stretch of world-class formations in any US national park.

Honourable Mentions

Five trails cannot cover a park this dense. Three more worth your time:

  • Park Avenue (1 mi one-way, easy) — a corridor between 300-foot fins near the entrance. Best walked one-way with a car shuttle to Courthouse Towers.
  • Sand Dune & Broken Arch loop (1.7 mi, easy) — reliably quiet parking even at midday, and a genuinely pleasant walk through grassland and fins.
  • Tower Arch (2.6 mi RT, moderate) — reached via the unpaved Salt Valley Road in the remote Klondike Bluffs. High clearance recommended, impassable when wet.

Want the Arch Almost Nobody Reaches?

Eye of the Whale Arch sits at the end of the Willow Springs 4WD road — deep sand, high clearance required, and near-total solitude inside one of America’s busiest parks.

What to Carry

Arches is high desert at roughly 5,000 feet with almost no shade anywhere. The gear list is short but non-negotiable.

  • Water — 2L minimum, 4L in summer. There is no water on any trail in this park. Fill at the visitor centre or Devils Garden.
  • Grippy soles. Slickrock is excellent traction when dry and unforgiving when dusty. Worn-smooth trainers are the most common cause of slips on the Delicate Arch ledge.
  • Sun hat, long sleeves, high-SPF sunscreen. Reflected UV off pale sandstone burns faster than direct sun alone.
  • Headtorch if hiking Delicate Arch at sunset. The slickrock descent in the dark is not navigable by phone light.
  • Offline map for Devils Garden and Fiery Furnace. Cell coverage inside the park is unreliable beyond the visitor centre.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the best hike in Arches National Park?

Delicate Arch, for almost everyone. It is 3.2 miles round trip with 629 feet of gain and finishes at the most recognisable formation in Utah. If you want something longer and harder, the Devils Garden Primitive Loop at 7.9 miles is the park’s most demanding marked trail.

Do you need a reservation to hike in Arches in 2026?

No timed-entry reservation is required to enter the park. The only hike needing advance booking is Fiery Furnace, which requires a self-guided permit or a ranger-led walk reserved through Recreation.gov.

How long does the Delicate Arch hike take?

Two to three hours round trip including time at the arch. Allow longer at sunset, when the descent is slower and the bowl is crowded.

Is the Devils Garden Primitive Loop dangerous?

It is the hardest marked trail in the park but not technical. The risks are route-finding on cairned slickrock, several unprotected fin traverses with drops, and one awkward downclimb. It becomes genuinely hazardous when wet — do the Double O out-and-back instead if rain has fallen recently.

Can you hike Arches in summer?

Yes, but only at dawn and dusk. Daytime highs reach 100°F and slickrock surface temperatures exceed 140°F with no shade on any major trail. Finish exposed hikes before 10 a.m. or start after 5 p.m.

Which Arches hikes are suitable for children?

Landscape Arch (1.9 mi, graded gravel), Double Arch (0.6 mi, flat sand), the Windows loop and the Sand Dune & Broken Arch loop are all family-friendly. Delicate Arch is manageable for older children but has an exposed final ledge.
